Designing Enterprise Software

Short description of the project

Research • UX Design • UI Design • Collaboration • End to End

Material worked on during this project is limited due to NDA. If you're interested in seeing more, I'm happy to walk through my process during a call.

I designed enterprise cybersecurity software for four years and led a variety of projects including designing various workflows to assist in data collection and processing, a complex rule builder, and data dense displays. These projects covered the entire design process from gathering research and requirements to providing development support, all while focusing on simplifying complex concepts into easier, user-friendly workflows.

While projects may require different approaches, there are a few things that remain constant throughout — designing in ambiguity, having limited access to end users, working with a wide range of stakeholders, and general takeaways of working on enterprise software.

Stakeholders on Stakeholders

This brings us to the next major point in this reflection exercise: the vast number of stakeholders. Even if I think I understand the problem and how we should tackle it to keep the user's interests in mind, I need to pitch my ideas and navigate through a range of stakeholders, such as the rest of my design team, product management, architecture, upper management, development, and sales engineers, to name a few. While some may describe getting the approval of all these groups is like trying to herd cats, I found it has become much easier as time goes on. For example, I change my presentation techniques based on my audience, finesse conversations to stay focused and productive, and navigate the tricky parameters of limited time, manpower, and existing architecture needed to bring my designs to life.
