Threat Intel Feeds

A feature integrating external data to assist security analysts

Research • UX Design • UI Design • Collaboration • End to End

Material worked on during this project is limited due to NDA. If you're interested in seeing more, I'm happy to walk through my process during a call.

Having usable data and being able to leverage it when needed is incredibly important in the cybersecurity world so security analysts are able to quickly and effectively respond to threats. During this project, I was tasked with designing a solution that allows users to integrate third party threat intelligence feeds and their data into our system to provide contextual data around possible threats.

Background

My Role: Primary UX Designer

Team: 1 UX Designer, 2 Project Managers, 2 Engineering Leads

Timeline: 2 months

Problem

In order to be most effective, security analysts need to be able to identify and respond to threats quickly. Due to the high volume of data coming through their system, this can be incredibly difficult without contextual data and knowledge of the situation. To help with this, I was tasked with designing the integration and display of external threat intel feeds in the system. After completion, a user should be able to...

  • Add and configure threat intel feeds
  • View flagged data from the feeds in both search and dashboards
  • Drill down on flagged data points to see more contextual data about it
  • Mark values as safe and be able to view and manage these values

Stakeholders

Team: While I was the only designer on this project and drove it forward, this project was unique due to the variety of areas in our system the final solution would be implemented and the amount of logistics involved to actually get it implemented in a meaningful way. I worked closely with two project managers that oversaw different areas of the platform along with two different engineering teams during this process. To help encourage transparent communication, I kept conversations in a group chat with PMs and engineering leads and scheduled frequent meetings when important information and decisions were being made for scope, requirements, and design direction.

Users:

  • Security analysts - need to make solution accessible to new and power users. They are interacting with solution in dashboards and search pages.
  • Platform administrators - will need to configure feeds and manage list that contains values marked as safe

Stakeholders

Gather Information: I began this design process by gathering information about threat intel feeds, how they are commonly used, and existing solutions to show contextual data. I used Figma to compile the information and whiteboard possible design ideas.

Scope Refinement: After gathering information, I presented my findings to the primary project manager. We discussed potential scope but ultimately decided that conducting some quick user research would help decide what features we would target first.

User Research: I conducted three user interviews with internal and external users to understand what data is most helpful to see from threat intel feeds and how users expect it to be displayed. I found people were most interested in...

  • general feed information (name of feed, date last updated, etc)
  • be able to quickly see that a data point is flagged
  • drill down to see more contextual data

Scope Refinement (Part 2): After conducting interviews and analyzing the data, I presented my findings and recommendations to the primary PM. We worked together to refine the scope of work based on this information, timeline, and estimated engineering resources.

Design: As this feature covers multiple steps and areas of the platform, I started my work by sketching out some possible ideas for configuring the threat intel feeds. Afterwards, I balanced moving between sketching the other related features and creating mock ups in Figma. I made sure to keep PMs and engineering leads updated throughout and made sure to iterate based on their concerns and feedback when necessary.

Validation: After designs were signed off by project management and engineering, I validated my designs by interviewing three users and getting their thoughts and feedback. This lead to tweaking some colors and UI elements slightly, but overall validated the workflow and presentation of threat intel feeds.

Outcome

While this project had many moving parts, I successfully designed the configuration and display of threat intel feeds in the system to assist security analysts in quickly and effectively respond to threats. While this work has not yet been implemented, designs have been approved and are ready to be picked up by engineering whenever they have the time.

  • 12 screens created
